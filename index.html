/What this is

A curve made of nodes joined in order. Two rules do all the work: when an edge stretches past a threshold a new node is inserted in the middle of it, and every node pushes away from any other node that comes too close.

Subdivision supplies material. Repulsion stretches the edges back past the threshold, so subdivision fires again. That feedback is the growth — the folds are what a curve does when it is forced to get longer inside a space that is not getting bigger.

The same process shapes cabbage leaves, coral, the lining of the gut and the folds of the cortex.

/The five rules

Subdivision
Splits any edge longer than the detail threshold. Always on — it is the only source of new nodes.
Repulsion
Every node pushes away from every other node within the radius, connected or not. Closer means stronger.
Attraction
Pulls each node toward its two neighbours. Without it, repulsion inflates the shape indefinitely.
Alignment
Pulls each node toward the midpoint of its neighbours, rounding off sharp corners.
Jitter
A small random nudge each step. It keeps knocking edges back over the split threshold so growth does not stall.

/Stages

The numbered presets switch the rules on one at a time, so each one isolates a single contribution. Stages 3 and 4 settle near an equilibrium where edges sit just under the split threshold and growth slows to a crawl — stage 5 is where it resumes.

/Open curves

Line and Arc seed an open curve rather than a closed ring. Endpoints are handled separately: they attract toward their single neighbour and are skipped by alignment, since an endpoint has no midpoint to align to. An open curve grows outward from its tips instead of ruffling inward.

/Performance

Repulsion is naturally O(n²) — every node against every other. This build buckets nodes into a spatial hash keyed on the repulsion radius and only checks the nine neighbouring cells, which keeps it near O(n) and holds a steady frame rate to several thousand nodes.
