what this is

A curve made of nodes joined in order. Two rules do all the work: when an edge stretches past a threshold a new node is inserted in the middle of it, and every node pushes away from any other node that comes too close.

Subdivision supplies material. Repulsion stretches the edges back past the threshold, so subdivision fires again. That feedback is the growth — the folds are what a curve does when it is forced to get longer inside a space that is not getting bigger.

The same process shapes cabbage leaves, coral, the lining of the gut and the folds of the cortex.

the rules

subdivision
Splits any edge longer than detail. Always on — the only source of new nodes.
repulsion
Every node pushes away from every other within radius, connected or not. Closer is stronger.
attraction
Pulls each node toward its two neighbours. Without it, repulsion inflates the shape forever.
alignment
Pulls each node toward the midpoint of its neighbours, rounding off sharp corners.
jitter
A small random nudge each step, so growth does not stall at equilibrium.

stages

The numbered presets turn the rules on one at a time, so each isolates a single contribution. Stages 3 and 4 settle where edges sit just under the split threshold and growth slows to a crawl. Stage 5 is where it resumes.

open curves

Line and arc seed an open curve rather than a closed ring. Endpoints attract toward their single neighbour and are skipped by alignment, since an endpoint has no midpoint to align to. An open curve grows outward from its tips instead of ruffling inward.

performance

Repulsion is naturally O(n²). Nodes are bucketed into a spatial hash keyed on the repulsion radius, so each only tests the nine cells around it — close to O(n), and steady at sixty frames a second to twelve thousand nodes.
